How to read this page (30 seconds)
  • “More likely” compares one group with everyone else. For example, 71% of one group agree with a statement, against 42% of everyone else.
  • Likely real means the difference is big enough that it is unlikely to be luck, even after allowing for how many comparisons we ran. Worth watching looks real but could still be chance. Hint is only a nudge to look closer.
  • We only compare groups with at least 5 people. Small groups swing wildly, so treat anything based on under 15 or 20 people as a lead to investigate, not a conclusion.
  • These patterns show what goes together, not what causes what. Being actively involved in church may not cause a view; the two may simply travel together.

Patterns we found

Explore a group

Build a group by tapping choices, for example 21–23 + actively involved + serious or engaged. Within a row, tapping several choices means “any of these”; across rows it means “and”. You'll see who they are and how they answer differently from everyone else.

Compare two questions

Pick any two things and see how they line up, such as church involvement against relationship stage.

Beliefs that go together

People who agree with one statement often agree (or disagree) with another. This shows those links.

Types of respondents

The page groups people whose agree/disagree answers look alike, without using age, church life or anything else. Then it describes who ended up in each group. Read the beliefs and give each group a name that fits.
